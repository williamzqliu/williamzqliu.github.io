This poster was my final project for Information Design Theory and Critical Thinking, with
course guidance from Professor Ashley Treni. The brief asked for one large poster explaining
information design to someone new to the field.

I made it for Chinese parents helping their children choose a university major. I wrote the
Chinese and English content, translated between the two, selected what to include, and laid
out both editions.

## Designing for parents

I wrote for parents who know majors like finance, engineering, and medicine but may not have
heard of information design. I organized the poster around questions they might ask: What
is it really about? What will your child learn? Is your child a good fit? What careers can
it lead to?

Version 1 opened with a definition of the field. In the final poster, I start from a
process parents already know: the left column follows the Gaokao and the application, from
the exam to the first day of university. At the step of exploring majors, a band crosses the
poster to information design and then to the paths it can lead to.

Feedback from parents who read the poster led me to add examples. Each principle in the
center column now carries an everyday case, such as a school file that lists the required
documents and deadlines. In the right column, I added real projects by other designers, such
as We Are Here and Project Re:form, with the roles each involved. Passages beginning *Your
child might…* sketch work a student could one day do.

I also drew lines between related blocks, connecting what the field is, what a student
learns, and where the work is applied, so that parents could build an overall picture of the
major.

## Finding the structure

Version 1 had a clear sequence of seven numbered questions along a winding path, but the
drawing dominated the page, and it read more like an illustration than a poster.

I then tried different ways of dividing the page into zones and connecting them. One draft
arranged the content horizontally; it did not work well, and I did not take it further.

In Version 2, the title ran vertically up the left edge, where it was easy to miss. In the
final poster, I set it horizontally at the top left. Parents' feedback had also led me to
rework the entry point and the reading flow, so I gave the Gaokao and application steps the
left column directly beneath the title, opening the poster on a situation parents know.
