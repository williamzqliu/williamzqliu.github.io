This identity for the Classical Gardens of Suzhou, a UNESCO World Heritage site, was my project for Graphic Design Synthesis at Northeastern University, taught by Douglass Scott. Over the semester, the course asked for a mark and logotype, typography and color, a fifth element, and a set of applications, among them stationery, an info sheet, a website, magazine ads, a vehicle, and a site sign.

I worked from the idea of "a garden within." The mark looks through a garden doorway, the patterns come from garden windows, and the ads and the website show photographs inside window shapes.

## The mark

The logomark is a view through a garden doorway, drawn in perspective under upturned eaves. Because of that perspective, the mark sits beside the name in a horizontal lockup and has no stacked version.

For the wordmark, I redrew the H, O, U, A, and R, and set the letters and their spacing on a grid whose unit is the typeface's horizontal stroke. The bilingual lockup adds the Chinese name, 苏州古典园林, beneath the English.

## Patterns from garden windows

The fifth element is a set of seven lattice patterns reproduced from windows in Suzhou's gardens. I redrew all seven on one square grid with 45-degree diagonals, so each can run as a narrow border or repeat across a whole surface.

Cinnabar appears once on each piece, as a single square or an L of three squares set into the lattice.

## On screen and on site

The website's home page sets the title beside a begonia window on a celadon ground of begonia lattice. The window frames the North Temple Pagoda as seen from the Humble Administrator's Garden.

For the vehicle, I chose a wooden tour boat and kept its bare wood. The name is painted in rice white near one end of the hull, and a pine-green begonia plaque hangs at the other.

The site sign is a row of eight freestanding, double-sided panels, each under a tiled roof above a band of lattice openwork. The first carries the title. The next six give the same text in Chinese, English, French, Spanish, Russian, and Arabic, each with its own lattice.

The last panel is an empty moon gate, with only the logo and the words "A garden within" below it. Where the other panels describe the gardens, this one frames part of them, the way the gardens' own moon gates and lattice windows frame a view. In the mockup at the top of this page, it stands nearest the viewer, and its circle takes in a weathered stone pillar against the gallery behind, so the scenery beyond the panel becomes part of the sign.
